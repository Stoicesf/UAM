\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We propose \emph{Dynamic Sparse Graph Fusion} (DSGF), a spatiotemporal guidance
module that injects coordination structure into decentralized MAPPO execution
under dynamic communication constraints.
DSGF consists of three coupled components:
(i)~quality-aware dynamic graph construction,
(ii)~sparse spatial attention over informative neighbors, and
(iii)~temporal residual fusion with a decaying residual policy head.
The resulting guidance field $\Phi_i^{t}$ conditions the local actor while the
critic remains a standard centralized MAPPO critic (CTDE).

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Consider $N$ cooperative UAVs navigating in a continuous 2D workspace under
partial observability.
At time $t$, agent $i$ receives a local observation
\begin{equation}
o_i^{t}
=
\bigl[
p_i^{t},\;
v_i^{t},\;
g_i^{t},\;
\ell_i^{t}
\bigr]
\in \mathbb{R}^{d_o},
\end{equation}
where $p_i^{t},v_i^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$ are position and velocity,
$g_i^{t}$ is the goal-relative vector, and $\ell_i^{t}$ denotes local range
sensing (e.g., lidar).
The decentralized policy produces a continuous action $a_i^{t}\in\mathcal{A}$.

Communication is range-limited: two agents may exchange latent messages only if
$\|p_i^{t}-p_j^{t}\|<R_c$, inducing a time-varying graph
$G_t=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_t)$.
The cooperative objective is to maximize the expected team return
\begin{equation}
\max_{\theta}
\;
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}
\Biggl[
\sum_{t=0}^{H}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
r_i^{t}
\Biggr],
\qquad
r_i^{t}
=
r_i^{\mathrm{goal}}(t)
+
r_i^{\mathrm{coll}}(t),
\end{equation}
subject to collision penalties and the communication topology $G_t$.
Long-horizon coordination requires information beyond immediate geometric
neighbors, yet dense all-to-all attention scales poorly and may overfit
surrogate alignment signals during prolonged training.

\subsection{Dynamic Sparse Graph Construction}
\label{sec:dyn-graph}

Given agent positions and velocities, we first build a hard communication mask
\begin{equation}
A_{ij}^{t}
=
\mathbb{1}\!\left[
\|p_i^{t}-p_j^{t}\| < R_c,\; i\neq j
\right].
\end{equation}
Unlike binary GAT graphs, DSGF further scores each feasible link by a
learnable communication quality $q_{ij}^{t}\in(0,1)$.
We form edge features
$e_{ij}^{t}=\bigl[d_{ij}^{t},\,\Delta v_{ij}^{t},\,\theta_{ij}^{t},\,c_{ij}^{t}\bigr]$,
where $d_{ij}$ is distance, $\Delta v_{ij}$ is relative speed magnitude,
$\theta_{ij}$ is velocity alignment, and
$c_{ij}=\exp\!\bigl(-d_{ij}^{2}/(2R_c^{2})\bigr)$ is a distance prior.
A small MLP maps $e_{ij}$ to quality scores $q_{ij}=\sigma(\mathrm{MLP}(e_{ij}))$.
The weighted adjacency used by attention is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{A}_{ij}^{t}
=
A_{ij}^{t}\, q_{ij}^{t}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\tilde{A}$ retains the sparsity of the radius constraint while
preferentially weighting informative neighbors (approaching / aligned agents)
over geometrically valid but weakly useful links.

\subsection{Spatial Sparse Attention}
\label{sec:sparse-attn}

Node states are embedded as $h_i^{(0)}=W_o o_i$.
Sparse multi-head attention aggregates only over the support of $\tilde{A}$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{t}
=
\frac{
\exp\!\bigl(f(h_i^{t},h_j^{t})\bigr)\,
\tilde{A}_{ij}^{t}
}{
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_i^{t}}
\exp\!\bigl(f(h_i^{t},h_k^{t})\bigr)\,
\tilde{A}_{ik}^{t}
},
\qquad
\mathcal{N}_i^{t}
=
\{j:A_{ij}^{t}=1\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_i^{\prime\,t}
=
\sigma\!\Biggl(
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i^{t}}
\alpha_{ij}^{t}\, W h_j^{t}
\Biggr).
\end{equation}
If $|\mathcal{N}_i^{t}|=\emptyset$, the node keeps its local embedding
(no hallucinated long-range messages).

\paragraph{Difference from GAT.}
Standard GAT treats all in-radius neighbors as equivalent binary edges.
DSGF modulates scores by $q_{ij}$, i.e., attention is conditioned on both
geometry \emph{and} instantaneous communication quality, which improves
robustness when $R_c$ is small (Sec.~\ref{sec:comm-results}).

\subsection{Temporal Residual Fusion}
\label{sec:temporal-residual}

Spatial features alone induce myopic policies.
We maintain a per-agent temporal state with a GRU cell,
\begin{equation}
z_i^{t}
=
\mathrm{GRU}\!\bigl(h_i^{\prime\,t},\, z_i^{t-1}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and emit a guidance field
$\Phi_i^{t}=\mathrm{MLP}(z_i^{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_\Phi}$
(normalized directional components plus risk/priority channels).

\paragraph{Residual policy decoupling.}
A key source of long-horizon degradation in guided MARL is coupling the
task reward with a strong guidance alignment surrogate.
DSGF therefore injects guidance as a \emph{residual correction} rather than a
dominant policy input:
\begin{equation}
a_i^{t}
\sim
\pi_\theta\!\bigl(\,\cdot\mid o_i^{t}\bigr)
\;+\;
\beta(t)\,
\Delta_\phi\!\bigl(\Phi_i^{t}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\beta(t)$ anneals from $\beta_{\max}$ to $\beta_{\min}$ over training.
This residual schedule prevents over-specialization to the guide field while
preserving early coordination benefits.
Empirically (4-UAV ablation, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), removing residual
decoupling collapses success from $9.27\%$ to $0.22\%$.

\subsection{DSGF-guided MAPPO Optimization}
\label{sec:opt}

Training follows CTDE MAPPO: the actor is decentralized and receives
$(o_i,\Phi_i)$, while the critic estimates $V_\psi(\mathbf{o})$ from joint
observations.
Policy updates maximize the clipped PPO objective with GAE advantages;
optional guidance alignment rewards (used by GAT baselines) are \emph{disabled}
for DSGF v2 so that task return remains the sole optimization signal for the
residual actor.
Checkpoints and hyperparameters are frozen in
\texttt{configs/dsgf/dsgf\_v2\_frozen.yaml}.

\subsection{Complexity Analysis}
\label{sec:complexity}

Let $d$ be the hidden width and $k=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i|\mathcal{N}_i|$ the average
degree under $R_c$.
Dense GAT / Transformer attention costs $O(N^{2}d)$ per layer.
DSGF message passing, restricted to the support of $\tilde{A}$, costs
$O(Nkd)$; the GRU update adds $O(Nd)$ per timestep.
Hence the dominant per-step guidance complexity is
\begin{equation}
O(Nkd+Nd)
\quad\text{with}\quad
k\ll N
\text{ under sparse connectivity}.
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:complexity} summarizes the comparison.
Pairwise distance evaluation for radius masking remains $O(N^{2})$ and is
shared by all geometric graph methods; the practical gain of DSGF lies in
\emph{attention} sparsity and quality weighting, which dominate wall-clock cost
once $N$ grows.
