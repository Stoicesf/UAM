\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We structure prior work around three gaps.
The unifying distinction is:
\emph{previous methods largely treat communication as given;
AC-DSGF treats communication as learned}.

\subsection{Multi-Agent RL for UAV Swarms}
Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) methods such as MAPPO,
MADDPG, and value-factorization approaches (e.g., QMIX) stabilize cooperative
control by sharing critics or mixing networks in training while keeping
actors local at execution~\cite{yu2022mappo,lowe2017maddpg,rashid2018qmix}.
Aerial applications demonstrate navigation and collision handling under partial
observability~\cite{chung2018uav,bettini2022vmas}.
These pipelines, however, typically optimize task return under an implicit
assumption of \textbf{full or sufficient communication}: either agents act
independently given a shared training signal, or coordination depends on
centralized features that do not survive bandwidth, energy, and latency limits
of large UAV swarms.
\begin{quote}
\emph{Gap~1.}
Existing MARL methods for UAV cooperation mainly optimize policy performance
while overlooking communication constraints in large-scale swarms.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Graph-based Communication and Interaction Modeling}
Graph neural networks and attention inject relational inductive bias into
MARL~\cite{jiang2018graphcomm,iqbal2019actorattention,jiang2020graph,velickovic2018gat}.
GAT-style guides and graph Transformers adaptively weight neighbors and capture
time-varying spatial relations.
Critically, \textbf{attention is not a communication decision}: it aggregates
features under a \emph{predefined} support---a radius mask, a dense graph, or a
hand schedule.
Dense message passing costs $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$; binary radius graphs still
exchange on \emph{all} in-range edges regardless of task urgency.
Our prior DSGF improves quality weighting and residual guidance, yet still
activates the full geometric support whenever $A_{ij}{=}1$.
\begin{quote}
\emph{Gap~2.}
Existing graph attention mechanisms mainly aggregate information assuming
predefined communication structures; they do not equate topology adaptation
with bandwidth-aware economy.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Communication-Efficient MARL}
A parallel literature studies message pruning, gated talk/silence decisions,
dropout, and bandwidth-aware objectives~\cite{singh2019learning,
wang2020learning,foerster2016learning,sukhbaatar2016commnet,das2019tarmac,
kim2019learning,mordatch2018emergent}.
Many approaches rely on \textbf{stochastic deletion}
$P(\mathrm{drop})$ or heuristic schedules weakly coupled to continuous
control.
Random pruning can discard coordination-critical links under tight budgets;
over-aggressive silence collapses information flow.
In contrast, AC-DSGF learns a continuous gate
$g_{ij}^{t}=\sigma(W[h_i,h_j,d_{ij},q_{ij}])$ jointly with the policy under
an explicit Soft Comm.\ Mass $C=\sum g_{ij}$ and Top-$K$ budgeting---i.e.,
\textbf{topology optimization} with selective interaction, not random dropout
or post-hoc pruning.
\begin{quote}
\emph{Gap~3.}
Prior communication-efficient MARL often \textbf{prunes messages} on a fixed
graph rather than \emph{learning the interaction topology} jointly with
cooperative control under a quantified activation budget.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Previous & Communication is \emph{given} (radius / dense / random drop) \\
\textbf{Ours} & Communication is \emph{learned} (task-aware gate + budget) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.25em}
\noindent
AC-DSGF jointly addresses
(i)~task performance,
(ii)~learned topology via $g_{ij}^{t}$, and
(iii)~explicit budgets $C=\sum g_{ij}$ with Top-$K$, while residual DSGF
guidance keeps sparse messaging assistive
(Secs.~\ref{sec:silence}--\ref{sec:trigger}).
It is therefore not ``DSGF + $\ell_2$ pruning,'' but a
communication-constrained coordination stack.
