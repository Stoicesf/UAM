\begin{abstract}
Existing multi-UAV swarm policies often treat communication topology as fixed
infrastructure (fully connected, radius, or $k$-nearest graphs).
Attention weights improve message aggregation but do not decide whether a link
should be activated under bandwidth limits---messages may still be transmitted
before weighting.
We study \emph{when and with whom} UAVs should communicate under soft budget
constraints, and propose \textbf{AC-DSGF}, which learns adaptive communication
sparsity patterns via Candidate Edge Scoring, Budget-Constrained Edge Selection,
and Residual Recovery.
A degree-constrained scaling bound shows $\eta_N=\mathcal{O}(1/N)$.
Under identical communication budgets, AC-DSGF achieves competitive coordination
while inducing substantially lower Soft Communication Activation Mass (SCAM)
across swarm scales.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We present AC-DSGF for learning adaptive communication sparsity patterns under
soft budgets for multi-UAV swarms.
Topology is treated as a decision variable and constructed via Candidate Edge
Scoring, Budget-Constrained Edge Selection, and Residual Recovery.
We provide a degree-constrained communication scaling bound
($\eta_N=\mathcal{O}(1/N)$), a residual \emph{action-stability} analysis, and an
\emph{interpretive} view of learned gates as an approximate induced budgeted
edge selection (not an explicit combinatorial solver).
Under identical communication budgets, AC-DSGF achieves competitive task
effectiveness with scalable and robust topology adaptation, with SCAM---not
brute-force Success---as the primary efficiency signal.
Current evaluation focuses on simulation-based multi-agent navigation;
packet-level radio metrics and hardware deployment remain future work.
